What the system does

One rigid transform, learned by a geometric neural network, conditioned on a fixed protein pocket.

The task is a deliberately narrowed version of molecular docking: given a protein pocket and a ligand's molecular graph, predict the rigid-body rotation and translation that carries the ligand from a random starting pose to its true bound pose. Ligand shape and pocket geometry are both held fixed — only the 6 rigid degrees of freedom (3 translation + 3 rotation) are generated. Data: from 19,037 complexes to a validated 800

Every candidate is parsed and checked; exclusions are counted and named, never silently dropped.

The user's own licensed PDBBind v2020.R1 archive (P-L.tar.gz, 19,037 protein–ligand complexes) is the sole data source — nothing redistributed, nothing re-downloaded from a mirror. A seeded sample of 1,200 candidates is drawn, covalent-flagged entries excluded up front (the rigid, non-covalent binding assumption doesn't apply to them). Every remaining candidate is independently parsed, featurized with RDKit + Biopython, and checked against a 100-atom ligand-size cap.

Of 1,200 candidates, 1,066 validated cleanly — every excluded complex failed for exactly one documented reason (ligand size), not a silent parse error. The dataset is capped at 800 and split by a deterministic, similarity-aware procedure: proteins grouped by exact sequence match, ligands grouped by RDKit Butina clustering on Morgan fingerprints (Tanimoto ≥ 0.4), any two complexes sharing either group merged by union–find into one leakage group. Whole groups — never individual complexes — are assigned to train/val/test, landing exactly on the 80/10/10 target across 437 groups.

Model evolution & the dead-zone ablation

Three architectures, one hypothesis test: does a hard interaction cutoff kill the training signal?

All three variants predict a single rigid-body velocity per complex by pooling per-atom "forces" into a net translation and torque (v_trans = mean(fᵢ), v_rot = mean(rᵢ×fᵢ)) — the cross product makes rotation a genuine SO(3) pseudovector, so the whole construction is provably E(3)-equivariant. The held-out generalization experiment

640 train / 80 validation / 80 test, disjoint by construction — the test set is touched exactly once.

This is the experiment the rest of the project earns: the soft-weighted model, architecturally unchanged, trained with mini-batches (size 16) for 150 epochs on 640 complexes, checkpointed by validation loss, and evaluated on 80 complexes it never saw — not in training, not in checkpoint selection. Best checkpoint: epoch 104, validation loss 12.50. Training took 6.3 minutes and peaked at 344 MB of GPU memory on the RTX 5080.

Engineering & theory behind it

What this project actually required, beyond calling a training loop.

SE(3) rigid-body parameterization

Poses as x = R·L + t; a target-pose-centered canonical shape so R₁=I by construction.

SO(3) geodesic interpolation

Rotation paths via matrix exponential of a constant angular velocity — the direct analogue of "linear path = constant velocity" on the rotation group.

Conditional Flow Matching

Regressing a constant target velocity field along a constructed probability path, applied to a non-Euclidean (SO(3)×R³) state space.

E(3)-equivariant message passing

Hand-written EGNN-style layers: scalar-gated relative vectors for translation, cross-product torque pooling for a provably equivariant rotation output.

Gaussian RBF distance encoding

Smooth basis-function featurization of interatomic distance, plus a custom envelope function diagnosed and fixed via a controlled ablation.

Dynamic graph construction

Protein–ligand interaction edges rebuilt from scratch every forward pass as the ligand pose moves.

Leakage-aware dataset splitting

Exact-sequence protein grouping, Butina/Tanimoto ligand clustering, union–find merge, deterministic proportional group assignment — no random per-complex split.

Cheminformatics & structural biology I/O

RDKit ligand parsing (SDF/MOL2 fallback), Biopython structure parsing, PDBBind archive/index handling at scale (19K complexes).

Training infrastructure

Mini-batch GPU training, validation-based checkpoint selection, single-pass held-out evaluation, GPU memory/timing instrumentation.

05

Limitations & future work

Stated plainly — no benchmark claims are made anywhere in this project.

What this v1 does not do

  • Rigid ligand, rigid pocket — no torsional or side-chain flexibility.
  • Small model, brief training — 64-dim hidden size, 150 epochs, 640 complexes.
  • Leakage control is a proxy — exact-sequence + fingerprint clustering, not a full published protocol.
  • No affinity signal used — purely structural (pose RMSD) training and evaluation.
  • 0% success at the strict 2Å threshold on held-out data — a working proof of concept, not a competitive pose predictor.

Natural next steps

  • Ligand torsional flexibility — rotatable-bond conformational change, not just rigid placement.
  • Protein-pocket flexibility — side-chain or backbone motion during docking.
  • Affinity / ranking heads — the PDBBind index already carries binding-affinity labels, unused so far.
  • Larger-scale virtual screening — ranking candidate ligand libraries against a target pocket.
